# Field Effect Brainstorm 2026-07-07 15:34

Scope: design-only heartbeat round. No source code changes. Three subagents each proposed three field effects using only formal base roles and existing official mechanisms.

This round intentionally avoided all implemented fields and all prior brainstorm families, including:

- frontline durability, caster haste, low-health brawl, backline hunt, DOT pressure, shield sustain, attack-speed frequency, control payoff;
- carry routing, multi-target pressure, status cleanse, mark economy, tactical windows;
- lane formation, lone-front formation, death revenge, first kill, last survivor, ultimate delay, cooldown tax, shield-break objective;
- guarded captain, ritual core, declared kill target, no-death contract, low-health fuse, tile explosions, neutral-object protection.

The prompt focused on remaining gaps:

## Consolidated Pass Candidates

### 1. Crownbreak Channel

Status: pass as the strongest boss-phase candidate.

Core idea:

At HP thresholds, the boss starts a visible channel. If the player deals enough damage during the channel, the boss is interrupted and exposed. If not, the boss completes a counterattack and gains a short threat window.

Suggested scope:

- Enemy boss only.

Suggested levels:

- L1: triggers once at 70% boss HP. 5-second channel. Interrupt by dealing 8% boss max HP during the channel. Success: boss takes +14% damage for 6 seconds. Failure: boss deals `power * 0.45` teamwide magic/fire/arcane damage and gains power +8% for 6 seconds.
- L2: triggers at 70% and 35%. 4.5-second channel. Interrupt threshold 10%. Success: boss takes +20% damage for 7 seconds. Failure: `power * 0.6` teamwide damage; boss power +12%, skill haste +8% for 7 seconds.
- L3: triggers at 75%, 45%, and 20%. 4-second channel. Interrupt threshold 12%. Success: boss takes +28% damage for 8 seconds. Failure: `power * 0.8` teamwide damage; boss power +18%, skill haste +12% for 8 seconds.

Favorable teams:

- Ranger / assassin / mage / warlock burst teams.
- Focus-fire teams that can save skills for boss channels.
- Priest / bard support shells that keep the burst carry alive until the channel.

Counter / risk:

- Slow DOT teams may fail the burst threshold unless values are tuned carefully.
- If the interrupt bar is not obvious, failure feels unfair.
- The threshold must be reachable by several team styles, not only one burst class.

Player understanding signal:

- Boss stops and starts a large channel bar under the HP bar.
- Damage fills an interrupt meter.
- Success makes the boss stagger and take larger damage numbers.
- Failure causes a clear cast completion and retaliation.

Difference from existing effects:

- Not `Arcane Tide` or `Banner Crucible`: the window comes from boss behavior, not player skill haste.
- Not `Decapitation Writ`: the target is always the boss body, not a selected enemy.
- Not `Glass Aegis`: no shield-break objective; it is an interrupt check.

Validation:

- Track interrupt success rate by archetype.
- Track whether failed channels correlate with clear team damage taken.
- Reject if only one burst archetype can pass.

### 2. Prism Reversal

Status: pass as the strongest weakness-discovery candidate.

Core idea:

Enemies enter resistance stances. Hitting into the resisted type deals reduced damage but builds cracks. When the cracks fill, the resistance flips into a vulnerability. The player learns that "bad damage now" can become "right damage if committed through the window."

Suggested scope:

- Enemy side for player-facing encounters.
- Can be mirrored in sandbox validation.

Suggested levels:

- L1: resisted type damage -18%. Hits of the resisted type build reversal. On fill, that type deals +25% damage for 4 seconds.
- L2: resisted type damage -25%. Reversal vulnerability +38% for 5 seconds.
- L3: resisted type damage -32%. Reversal vulnerability +55% for 5 seconds.

Favorable teams:

- High-frequency physical teams: ranger / assassin / berserker can crack armor stances.
- Spell teams: mage / warlock / alchemist can crack magic stances.
- Mixed teams: one side cracks while the other exploits ordinary windows.
- Priest / bard / knight shells help survive until reversal.

Counter / risk:

- Players may stop attacking when damage numbers are low, missing the crack-to-reversal idea.
- Damage reduction should not become immunity.
- Needs stance color, crack buildup, and reversal state visuals.

Player understanding signal:

- Low damage numbers appear during resistance.
- Each hit adds visible cracks.
- At reversal, the same damage type suddenly produces brighter, larger numbers.
- Failure means the player repeatedly starts cracking a stance but never finishes it or has no burst after reversal.

Difference from existing effects:

- Not a passive resistance or cleanse field.
- Not fixed tactical timing; the player actively flips the enemy state.
- Not a single-class counter because high-frequency physical, spell, and mixed teams can all use it.

Validation:

- Track crack completion rate.
- Track damage during resisted phase versus reversal phase.
- Check whether at least two team families gain meaningful but not universal uplift.

### 3. Battle Scar Index

Status: pass as the strongest loot-diagnosis candidate.

Core idea:

Combat itself remains fair. After victory or near-clear failure, the system reads one or two clear weaknesses and biases the next loot choices toward repair directions. The fight tells the loot system what the player should try next.

Suggested scope:

- Encounter reward rule, not a combat buff.

Suggested levels:

- L1: read the most obvious weakness; replace one loot option with a repair direction. 60% repair-target weight.
- L2: read two weaknesses; at least one option in a three-choice reward points at repair. 75% repair-target weight.
- L3: read two weaknesses and allow one option to lock a primary-stat direction. 90% repair-target weight.

Weakness mapping examples:

- Frontline dies before 12 seconds: HP / armor / shield / received healing.
- Lowest-health unit repeatedly fails rescue: healing / shield / guard-style equipment.
- Enemy backline survives too long and deals high damage: physical power / attack speed / focus equipment.
- Fight times out while player team stays alive: power / skill haste / DOT payoff.
- Burn / poison drives the loss: effect resist / cleanse-support direction.

Favorable teams:

- Any team in progression that is close enough to reveal a real failure reason.
- Especially useful for players stuck at a dungeon tier without knowing what to farm.

Counter / risk:

- Players may try to game the index by intentionally causing one failure type.
- Mitigate by requiring the weakness to affect outcome or repeat across fights.
- Do not output a confident diagnosis when evidence is weak; use generic foundational stats instead.

Player understanding signal:

- The player sees the fight problem, then the reward pool responds to that problem.
- A front collapse leads to survival-oriented choices.
- Timeout leads to output-oriented choices.
- Status deaths lead to effect-resistance or cleanse-support choices.

Difference from existing effects:

- Not a battle buff.
- Not `Unbroken Tithe`: it does not reward no deaths; it turns observed problems into loot direction.
- Not `Last Spark Fuse`: rescue is only one possible diagnosis, not the field's whole identity.

Validation:

- Track whether recommended loot direction changes the next fight's relevant signal.
- Reject if it always recommends generic power or HP.
- Check exploit cases where intentional failure steers high-value loot.

### 4. Rift Triage

Status: pass/record as a lighter weakness-diagnosis field.

Core idea:

Every few seconds, the game checks which damage type contributed least but still appeared. That type becomes a short vulnerability on the enemy. This rewards players for bringing small off-type probes.

Suggested levels:

- L1: every 8 seconds, least-contributing present type gains +18% damage for 4 seconds.
- L2: every 7 seconds, +28% for 5 seconds.
- L3: every 6 seconds, +42% for 5 seconds.

Reason to record:

- Good diagnostic logic, but it may teach "bring all three damage buckets" too strongly.
- It is less dramatic than `Prism Reversal`.

### 5. Ember Second Phase

Status: record as a simple boss-phase rule.

Core idea:

At a fixed time, the boss becomes more dangerous but easier to kill. It asks whether the player can survive phase one and finish phase two.

Suggested levels:

- L1: at 22 seconds, boss power +12%, attack speed +8%, armor -10%, received healing -20%.
- L2: at 20 seconds, power +18%, attack speed +12%, skill haste +8%, armor -16%, received healing -30%.
- L3: at 18 seconds, power +26%, attack speed +18%, skill haste +12%, armor -24%, received healing -40%.

Reason to record:

- Very readable, but less diagnostic than `Crownbreak Channel`.
- Can become a generic timer if not paired with boss visuals.

### 6. Forked Tribute Chest

Status: record as a reward-choice variant.

Core idea:

After victory, rewards split between "sharpen what worked" and "repair what nearly failed."

Suggested levels:

- L1: two-choice reward: highest contribution direction or biggest risk direction.
- L2: three-choice reward: highest contribution, biggest risk, second contribution.
- L3: three-choice reward where one option keeps slot type but changes stat tendency.

Reason to record:

- Strong for loot UX, but it is more reward UI than field effect.
- May be better integrated into the broader loot loop after `Battle Scar Index`.

## Duplicate / Rejected Items

- `Wound Ledger` overlaps with `Rift Triage` and `Battle Scar Index`. It is a good diagnostic UI idea but less field-like.
- `Dual-Phase Carapace` overlaps heavily with `Prism Reversal`. Keep its boss-phase flavor as a possible boss-specific variant, but do not count it as a separate direction.
- `Failure Triage Writ` overlaps with `Battle Scar Index`; merge its repeat-failure logic into the index rather than keeping it separate.

## Empty / Underexplored Directions

Worth asking for next:

- Enemy behavior-rule fields that change targeting preference without adding new skills.
- Encounter rules that teach "overkill waste" or "damage distribution waste" without repeating Many-Target Hall.
- Fields that test front/back transition after the frontline dies, if targeting hooks are reliable.
- Long-term dungeon modifiers that persist for several fights and teach route planning, not only single-fight solving.

## Recommended Priority

1. `Crownbreak Channel`: strongest boss-phase test and very readable.
2. `Prism Reversal`: strongest damage-type / window-learning test.
3. `Battle Scar Index`: strongest link between combat diagnosis and loot loop.
4. `Rift Triage`: useful lighter diagnostic if `Prism Reversal` is too hook-heavy.
5. `Ember Second Phase`: simple boss phase, good fallback.
